### EMG Signal Generation, Noise Addition, and Filtering Experiment:

<b>STEP 1</b>: Select an <b>EMG Dataset</b> from the dropdown menu. Adjust the <b>Duration</b> and <b>Sampling Rate</b> sliders as required. Click on the <b>"Generate EMG Signal"</b> button to visualize the original unfiltered EMG signal.
<br>
<br>

<b>STEP 2</b>: Select one or more noise types such as <b>Baseline Drift</b>, <b>Powerline Noise</b>, <b>Muscle Noise</b>, or <b>Motion Artifact</b> using the checkboxes. Click on the <b>"Add Noise to Signal"</b> button to observe the corrupted EMG signal.
<br>
<br>

<b>STEP 3</b>: Configure the filter settings in the <b>Filter Design</b> section by selecting the filtering <b>Algorithm (IIR/FIR)</b>, choosing the <b>Filter Type</b> (Low-pass, High-pass, Band-pass, etc.), and setting appropriate <b>Cutoff Frequencies</b> and <b>Filter Order</b>.
<br>
<br>

<b>STEP 4</b>: Click on the <b>"Apply Filter"</b> button to process the noisy EMG signal. Compare the <b>Filtered EMG</b> signals with the <b>Noisy</b> signals to evaluate the effectiveness of the applied filter.
<br>
<br>
<b>STEP 4: </b>Click the <b>Compute PSD</b> button to visualize the Power Spectral Density (PSD) of the Noisy, and Filtered signals. Observe the frequency components and compare the filter's effect on the signal's spectrum.
